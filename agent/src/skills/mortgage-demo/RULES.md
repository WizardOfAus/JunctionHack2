# Lending rules and checks in Finland (November 2026)

mortgage_calculator applies the rules marked (tool). Use the rest to explain.

## Lending rules
- Loan cap (tool): the loan can be at most 95% of the price for all buyers since 30 June 2026 (FIN-FSA, temporary).
  So the buyer needs at least 5% of their own money.
- Stress test (tool): the bank calculates the payment at 6% interest over at most 25 years. All debt payments at
  that rate should be at most 60% of net income (FIN-FSA). The housing company finance charge counts as debt.
- Dataset rule (tool): the synthetic S-Pankki applications use required income = the 6%, 25-year payment divided
  by 0.35. It is stricter than the bank rule, and it explains the declined applications in the dataset.
- Transfer tax (tool): 1.5% of the debt-free price for housing company shares. No first-time buyer exemption since
  1 January 2024 (Vero).
- Maximum loan term: 40 years since 1 June 2026. The stress test still uses at most 25 years.
- State guarantee (Valtiokonttori): covers up to 20% of the loan, at most EUR 60,000; fee 2.5% of the guaranteed amount.
- ASP loan: ASP savings of 10% of the price and at least 20 deposits; loan at most EUR 230,000 for one buyer in
  Helsinki, Espoo or Vantaa; the state pays 70% of the interest above 3.8% for 10 years.

## Housing company checks (asunto-osakeyhtiö)
- The debt-free price (velaton hinta) is the true price: selling price plus the housing company loan share.
- The finance charge (rahoitusvastike or pääomavastike) pays that loan. It can go up when rates go up.
- Planned renovations: a pipe renovation (putkiremontti) in a building from the 1960s to 1980s can cost hundreds
  of euros per m². Facade, roof, balcony and window work also cost a lot.
- Ground lease (vuokratontti): the plot rent is in the charges and can go up.
- Energy class: A is best, G is weakest.

## Next steps to give the user
- Ask two or three banks for a written offer, and compare the effective rate (todellinen vuosikorko).
- Before an offer on a home, read the property manager's certificate (isännöitsijäntodistus), the maintenance plan
  (kunnossapitotarveselvitys), the financial statements and the minutes of the last general meetings.
- Keep the safety buffer after buying.
